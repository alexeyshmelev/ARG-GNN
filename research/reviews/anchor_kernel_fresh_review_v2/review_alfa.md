### Summary

The manuscript proposes a neural architecture for persistent sampled haplotypes across changing marginal genealogies. It uses age-derived electrical resistances, Kron reduction to the sampled interface, a graph-local source encoder, and repeated bidirectional actions of graph-conditioned linear-process kernels on 32 fixed genomic anchors. It adds an integrated age-resistance bank and reference-inclusive normalization. The principal established results are conditional mathematical properties: PSD of the unnormalized covariance kernel, bounded normalized aggregation, and invariance to redundant graph-record and serial-edge subdivision. The reported numerical evidence is an untrained three-hold operator-order probe, with reversed-order Frobenius differences of 0.278, 0.185 and 0.109 and subdivision discrepancies below 4.1e-14. The document explicitly describes ongoing validation-only comparisons and supplies no trained ancestry or forecasting performance results. Its current contribution is therefore a methods construction and evaluation proposal, whose scientific utility and distinction from strong existing graph-kernel/state-space constructions remain unestablished.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue — The identifiable architectural differences from established dynamic graph kernels and graph state-space models have not been translated into a demonstrated contribution.**

   **Where —** Section 1, Sections 3–4, and Section 5.1, especially source lines 29, 82–104 and 201.

   **Why it matters —** The related-work paragraph correctly acknowledges Kron reduction, its prior use in GNN pooling, graph SPDE kernels and graph SSMs, but naming them does not establish what this combination contributes. The fixed-operator limit of the proposed propagator is a heat/linear-process kernel. Non-separable graph kernels with matrix-scaled stochastic forcing are already developed in Nikitin et al. [2111.08524], Section 4.4. Kernels and exact estimators for changing topologies are already developed in Romero et al. [1612.03615], Section V-B. GraphSSM [2406.00943], Eq. (5), already gives piecewise continuous graph-conditioned dynamics, although its graph dependence enters input filtering rather than the memory generator. That is a real distinction from the present changing C_QQ generator and should be explained precisely. MP-SSM [2505.18728], Eqs. (2)–(4), already places linear propagation of persistent states inside message passing and stacks nonlinear blocks. The covariance proof and semigroup property are valid established tools, rather than evidence that the resulting learned model extracts information unavailable to those constructions. No result currently answers the manuscript's own question about useful memory beyond the established components.

   **What would address it —** Give an equation-level comparison identifying state space, graph dependence in generator versus source, coordinate durations, query/reference treatment, source measure, and nonlinear depth. State a narrow contribution claim around the combination actually implemented. Include a baseline consisting of the same reduced boundary graphs and source encoder with a conventional bidirectional linear-process kernel/SSM, together with the planned GraphSSM adaptation and an MP-SSM comparator or a concrete explanation of why it is unsuitable. Equalize information, duration handling, tuning budget and nonlinear capacity. A gain attributable to the specified architectural difference, or a clear new representational/algorithmic guarantee that those methods lack, is needed to make the integration a research contribution.

2. **Issue — The manuscript currently contains no evidence that the proposed construction improves a learned inference task.**

   **Where —** Abstract, Figure 1, the paragraph following Proposition 3, and Section 5; trained results and comparison tables are absent.

   **Why it matters —** Figure 1 confirms expected consequences of noncommuting matrix products and the semigroup identity. It does not show that interval order, query coupling, age geometry, or kernel depth helps ancestry prediction. A nonzero propagator difference can occur even when both orders are equally uninformative about the target. The abstract's explicit working-document qualification is appropriate, but the planned controls cannot be evaluated as completed evidence. The same limitation applies to the proposed Chickenpox study. There are no effect sizes, uncertainty intervals, training failures, calibration values, or end-to-end costs in the supplied document.

   **What would address it —** Report completed, paired comparisons for a small predeclared set of primary hypotheses, with parent-level NLL and accuracy, calibration, variability across fitting seeds, and uncertainty across independent simulated parents. Report true-tree and reconstructed-tree settings separately on matched examples. Include the promised preparation, training, memory and parameter costs, counting Schur solves and eigensystem preparation rather than only cached propagation. Present neutral or negative results if they occur. Until those results exist, preserve the proposal framing and avoid interpreting Figure 1 as evidence of ancestry utility or architectural superiority.

3. **Issue — The proposed ablations do not yet fully distinguish age-dependent reference evidence and extra nonlinear capacity from useful graph-conditioned memory.**

   **Where —** Section 2.3, Eqs. (10), (15) and (17), and Section 5.1, especially source lines 195–199.

   **Why it matters —** The matched scalar-age construction is a useful attenuation control, but changing age geometry changes both C_QQ in the propagator and C_QR in reference injection and normalization. An age-branch gain could therefore come entirely from a better instantaneous reference map, without improved transport of previous query context. One versus two kernel layers also changes learned projections, gates and parameter count, so recording that count alone does not attribute a gain to repeated kernel exchange. The scalar propagation comparison changes diagonal decay as well as cross-query transport, as the manuscript itself acknowledges. The raw diagonal and gain-matched diagonal controls are consequently necessary. Moreover, D K^{-1} introduces graph-dependent spatial mixing at each source: it preserves homogeneous continuous-forcing DC gain, but does not make the transient action of the finite anchor measure identical.

   **What would address it —** Independently vary plain versus age geometry in the generator and in the reference source map, always transforming the matching ones source consistently. Compare two kernel layers against a one-kernel architecture with matched extra pointwise residual/gated capacity. Report the raw diagonal and gain-matched diagonal comparisons alongside the scalar comparison, and state exactly which source mixing each retains. Include a strong feature-only or shared-encoder-only predictor because the source bank already contains nearest-reference and fixed-kernel evidence. Interpret each contrast according to the mechanism it actually isolates, rather than treating any full-versus-scalar improvement as a uniquely temporal graph-memory result.

4. **Issue — The ancestry protocol is insufficient for confirmatory conclusions after architecture and checkpoint selection on four validation parents.**

   **Where —** Section 5.2, source lines 204 and 208; a frozen ancestry test protocol and statistical analysis plan are absent.

   **Why it matters —** Splitting parents before windows is the correct protection against a major leakage route. However, eight windows, 128 coordinates and multiple haplotypes do not turn four validation parents into a large independent evaluation sample. Within-parent predictions are correlated. Selecting checkpoints by validation NLL and comparing a factorial family of architectures on the same validation cohort also reuses that cohort for several decisions. The separately seeded 64-query cohort is not specified as a frozen test cohort and could be another tuning exercise. No primary contrast, uncertainty estimator or criterion for meaningful improvement is provided. Since the draft does not report significance claims, this is a limitation of the proposed inference protocol, not evidence that a reported p-value is wrong.

   **What would address it —** Freeze the principal endpoint, contrasts, tuning budget and selected configurations before generating or opening an independent ancestry test cohort. Treat parents as the principal sampling units and report paired parent-level effects with uncertainty. Distinguish biological simulation variability from optimizer-seed variability. State how many independent parents are needed to resolve the smallest effect of interest and how exploratory comparisons are distinguished from confirmatory ones. Specify whether the larger interface is evaluated with a fixed trained model or separately trained models, since those answer different generalization questions.

5. **Issue — The biological interpretation of the age geometry and the ancestry target lacks the definition and robustness evidence needed to support an ancestry-method claim.**

   **Where —** Section 2.3's ancestry-horizon motivation and Section 5.2's AncientEurope and Relate descriptions, source lines 57–77 and 204–206.

   **Why it matters —** Positive resistances and electrical equivalence do not establish that the induced cross-sample smoothing is appropriate for local ancestry. The four-way target is described as a census-lineage label, but the census horizon, label-assignment rule, reference ages/populations and panel selection are not stated. Those details define the task itself. The age scales 350 and 1400 generations and attenuation range 1–4 are fixed without a biological rationale or sensitivity study. On a single demographic simulator, an apparent benefit could reflect alignment with its particular event times or a favorable reference panel. Relate adds inferred-topology and age error, but the draft presents neither a paired effect of that error nor a definition of how observed age uncertainties are handled.

   **What would address it —** Define the target horizon and complete label-generation and panel-selection rules without exposing targets to input construction. Explain the relation of the resistance scales to that horizon and test a small, predeclared sensitivity range. Evaluate at least an independently simulated demographic perturbation and a reference-composition or reference-age perturbation, with matched true versus Relate-reconstructed inputs. Include tract-boundary or tract-length diagnostics in addition to aggregate coordinate scores where those are relevant to the claimed application. Limit any biological conclusion to the simulator and observation regime actually evaluated unless stronger evidence is supplied.

6. **Issue — Exact action on 32 anchors does not establish that the finite source representation captures relevant genomic variation or that predictions are stable under reasonable tiling choices.**

   **Where —** Sections 3.2–3.3, Proposition 3, and Figure 1, particularly source lines 107, 153 and 176.

   **Why it matters —** The scan is exact for the declared atomic source measure, and the subdivision proof is correct with anchors and tile endpoints fixed. These facts give no approximation guarantee for the graph-local signal between anchors. A short ancestry tract or a reference-evidence change can fall between all 32 source points. The operator still sees intervening graphs, but it receives no corresponding source impulse. Repartitioning a chromosome into different tiles also changes anchors, source masses, boundary context and potentially predictions, while lying outside Proposition 3. The manuscript does not specify whether x and Delta are measured in bases, megabases or tile-normalized coordinates. Because the responses are dimensionless, this choice determines the units and effective range of lambda and delta. Equal initial rates of 5, 20 and 80 are uninterpretable without that convention.

   **What would address it —** State the coordinate transformation, tile length, units of masses and rates, and boundary handling. Sweep anchor resolution and at least one anchor offset or tile-boundary choice on fixed held-out inputs, including short-tract and breakpoint-adjacent outputs. Report accuracy, prediction disagreement and cost as resolution increases. If no continuum approximation claim is intended, explicitly retain the finite-source interpretation and describe the resolution dependence as a modeling choice.

7. **Issue — Important construction and evaluation details are asserted to be recorded but are not supplied in a form that permits replication or auditing of the stated guarantees.**

   **Where —** Sections 2.2, 3.2, 4.2 and 5.2, source lines 46–53, 107–143, 181 and 208–210.

   **Why it matters —** The 47-feature bank is described by categories rather than formulas. “Source-conditioned,” “reference fractions” and “fixed kernel evidence” can encode substantial task-specific machinery and require explicit definitions to verify that query labels and hidden annotations are excluded. Proposition 3 is conditional on invariant features, so excluding the immediate-parent branch length alone does not certify the complete chosen bank. The manuscript gives neither encoder widths/depth, complete optimization and seed settings, nor an accessible mapping from its comparisons to the cited hashes or implementation tests. The forecasting transfer is also underspecified: the symmetric kernel uses sources on both sides of a readout, so all source coordinates and source features must be confined to the declared 52-week history for every one-week-ahead forecast. The text does not state the query/reference mapping or anchor/readout domain for that task. This is an unresolved audit question, not proof of forecasting leakage.

   **What would address it —** Supply a compact appendix or linked immutable configuration with every feature formula and normalization, the initialization of anchor and non-anchor readout embeddings, graph-encoder equations and zero-degree handling, optimizer settings, seeds and comparison identifiers. Provide reproducible tests for the actual feature bank's serial-insertion invariance, ordered noncommuting scans, self counting, gradients and numerical mass rejection, with precision and tolerances. Define Chickenpox samples explicitly, including source timestamps and features available at prediction time, loss, query/reference roles and chronological evaluation. State how numerical mass failures are handled and counted. A claim that records exist is not a substitute for making the needed records identifiable.

### Minor concerns

- Section 2.2 defines F_g^Q and U_g^R, while Section 3.2 starts from U^0_{Q,a} and U^0_{R,a}. State whether the initial anchor embeddings are H_g, these bounded projections, or another representation, and define U^0(x) for arbitrary readouts.
- Use distinct symbols for the persistent interface S and the number of geometry branches S in the complexity expression, and distinguish Q as a vertex set from its cardinality.
- In Proposition 1, state T(x,y)' = -K(x)T(x,y), T(y,y)=I, and identify the left domain endpoint at which the initial covariance is I. The current proof is correct under that standard interpretation.
- The manuscript correctly limits the PSD claim to the underlying symmetric kernel. Keep that qualification explicit in future claims: row normalization, reference source maps and the nonlinear residual network are not thereby PSD graph kernels across different tree-sequence inputs.
- ARGMix appears in Section 5.1 without a corresponding citation or definition in the supplied bibliography. Specify the cited method and the exact adaptation.
- The SPDE Chickenpox reference [2111.08524] reports interpolation and four/six-week extrapolation settings. A one-week forecast can be a useful new comparison, but published scores from those settings are not directly comparable to the protocol here.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — The conditional mathematical construction is sound, but the current working draft lacks the comparative evidence, attribution controls and complete task definitions needed to establish a distinct and useful research contribution.
