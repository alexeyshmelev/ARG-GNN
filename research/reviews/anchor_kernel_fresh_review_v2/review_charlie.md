### Summary

This working methods paper proposes inference on changing marginal genealogies by Kron-reducing anonymous ancestors to a persistent sampled interface, applying a graph-local encoder, and repeatedly exchanging bounded query/reference evidence through symmetric, graph-conditioned kernels at 32 fixed genomic anchors. Integrated age potentials preserve serial-edge equivalence, time-ordered propagators handle noncommuting graph holds, and a matching ones-source scan normalizes messages. The strongest argument is a coherent representation contract: under the explicit assumptions, subdivision of graph records and insertion of redundant serial ancestors leave predictions unchanged, while the completed unnormalized kernel has a covariance-based PSD certificate. I find the main mathematical arguments plausible as written. The reported numerical evidence is an untrained synthetic operator-order/subdivision probe. The ancestry and Chickenpox sections describe evaluation protocols, not demonstrated predictive results, and the abstract appropriately labels the document as validation-only work in progress. The construction is therefore promising, but its biological usefulness, resolution dependence, comparative advantage, and broader applicability remain open.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue — The current evidence does not establish validity for ancestry inference under reconstructed genealogies or for a broader class of changing-graph tasks.**  
   **Where —** Abstract, Section 1, Section 5.2 (source lines 204–210), and Figure 1.  
   **Why it matters —** The strongest case is that the authors have built a representation that respects two genuine nuisance equivalences. Those equivalences do not imply that electrical boundary geometry retains the information needed for local ancestry when branch ages and topology are estimated imperfectly. The only numerical result is homogeneous propagation on an untrained synthetic sequence. No ancestry accuracy, NLL, calibration, paired true-tree versus Relate-tree comparison, or held-out predictive result appears. The exploratory ancestry cohort contains 16 training parents and four validation parents under one AncientEurope_4A21 model, and the larger-query cohort is separately seeded. Increasing the query interface within this setting does not establish demographic or reconstruction robustness. Chickenpox has fixed topology and cannot by itself validate the changing-operator contribution. This is an evidence gap, not an allegation that missing experiments failed.  
   **What would address it —** First complete a frozen, previously unseen parent-level ancestry evaluation with paired true and reconstructed input trees, reporting differences and uncertainty for the main baselines. Then prioritize one controlled demographic mismatch and one realistic reconstruction/reference-panel perturbation. Identify the deployment scope these experiments support. For the interface-size comparison, evaluate nested query subsets of the same held-out parents so that Q = 8 versus Q = 64 is not confounded with a different simulated cohort. Treat Chickenpox as evidence for historical temporal aggregation unless a genuinely changing-graph external task is also tested.

2. **Issue — The biological target and observation model are not specified precisely enough to interpret success or audit the information budget.**  
   **Where —** Sections 1, 2.2, 2.3, and 5.2, especially “external census-lineage targets,” “47-feature ancestry bank,” and the fixed age scales 350 and 1400 generations.  
   **Why it matters —** Census-lineage ancestry is a specific simulator-defined target, rather than a self-evident observational definition of ancestry. The draft does not state the census time or population mapping, how lineages at migration boundaries are labeled, which samples form the labeled panel, or how their ages and population labels are chosen. The 47 features are summarized by names rather than formulas. “Source-conditioned observed common-ancestor times,” “reference fractions,” “nearest-reference tie votes,” and “fixed kernel evidence” could contain substantial task-specific ancestry information. Reference labels are legitimate inputs here, but their availability and processing must be matched across methods. The paper asserts exclusion of query targets and hidden annotations, yet the text alone cannot establish the exact separation. Likewise, the biological relationship between the ancestry horizon and the two age potentials is left unspecified. There is no need to assume leakage to recognize that the proposed task is currently underdefined.  
   **What would address it —** Add a target-and-input specification with census time, population codes, query/reference sampling ages and counts, generation-time convention, and exact lineage-label rules. Enumerate every feature, its formula, transformations, and permitted inputs, including how censored or missing common ancestors are handled. Distinguish true observed ages in the simulation from ages inferred by Relate or assumed known at deployment. Explain whether the age scales were chosen before comparisons and provide a horizon/scale sensitivity check. Include feature-only and graph-local comparisons with the same reference information, and a reduced feature bank, to determine whether the kernel adds information beyond these engineered summaries.

3. **Issue — Exactness for a fixed anchor measure leaves a consequential genomic-resolution and coordinate-scale question unanswered.**  
   **Where —** Sections 3.1–3.3, Proposition 3, Figure 1, and the 1 Mb/eight-window protocol in Section 5.2.  
   **Why it matters —** The text carefully limits its exactness claim to the predeclared finite measure. That qualification is mathematically appropriate, but it leaves the scientific choice A = 32 unsupported. Local source evidence from an interval with no anchor is absent from the kernel source sum, even though that interval can still affect propagation and a readout's graph-local embedding. Short ancestry tracts and reference relations near anchor or tile boundaries may therefore behave differently from long tracts. Subdivision invariance holds with tile endpoints and anchors fixed, and says nothing about shifting the grid, changing tile endpoints, or changing A. There is also no explicit coordinate-unit convention: Figure 1 spans 0–1, while the biological domain is given in base pairs and rates begin at 5, 20, and 80. The exponential uses rate times duration, so per-tile normalization versus a fixed genomic unit changes the meaning of memory length, especially when Relate coverage clips tiles.  
   **What would address it —** Define the coordinate transformation, rate units, anchor masses, and treatment of variable-length/clipped tiles. Compare several anchor resolutions and a shifted-grid replicate on the same frozen examples, reporting predictive differences, runtime, and performance by ancestry-tract length and distance from tile edges. State clearly whether the method targets a particular tile scale or a consistent physical genomic bandwidth. If approximation to a continuous source field is intended, state the regularity assumptions and supply an appropriate error argument. Otherwise retain the finite-measure claim and demonstrate that its selected resolution is adequate for the task.

4. **Issue — The planned ablations do not yet isolate the claimed benefits of kernel depth and spatial state transfer under the actual finite-anchor architecture.**  
   **Where —** Section 5.1 (source lines 195–201), particularly the scalar, raw-diagonal, and gain-matched diagonal controls.  
   **Why it matters —** The draft commendably acknowledges that replacing C_QQ by I changes both spatial coupling and decay. The additional controls improve the design, but “gain-matched” refers specifically to homogeneous continuous-forcing DC gain. Under a constant K, diagonal propagation with source D K^{-1} S has the same infinite-horizon continuous-forcing gain as full propagation. The implemented model instead has finitely many impulses, finite tiles, varying holds, and nonlinear depth updates. The DC argument does not match these finite-frequency or boundary behaviors. Moreover D K^{-1} already mixes query sources at each anchor, so this control removes coupling between propagated states while retaining a new form of spatial source mixing. This is a useful control, but not a general spatially independent comparator. The one-versus-two-layer contrast also changes parameter count, as the paper explicitly notes. Recording counts alone does not separate depth from capacity.  
   **What would address it —** Derive the exact finite-anchor action of each control and label which mechanism it removes and retains. Include a simple homogeneous finite-tile diagnostic showing residual gain/transient differences instead of presenting DC matching as full equivalence. Use both an independent temporal model with an identical local source encoder and the source-mixed diagonal control. Add a parameter-matched wider one-layer model or a shared-parameter depth variant. Freeze a primary depth/coupling/age contrast, give all methods comparable tuning and training budgets, and report interaction effects rather than selecting whichever contrast is favorable.

5. **Issue — The scientific novelty remains a combination of established ingredients without a demonstrated reason that this combination is necessary.**  
   **Where —** Section 1, Sections 3–4, and Section 5.1's architecture baselines.  
   **Why it matters —** The paper appropriately cites the antecedents and does not claim to have invented Kron reduction or covariance kernels. However, nonseparable graph covariance from stochastic dynamics is already developed in Non-separable Spatio-temporal Graph Kernels via SPDEs [2111.08524], and kernels on time-varying topologies appear in Kernel-based Reconstruction of Space-time Functions on Dynamic Graphs [1612.03615]. GraphSSM [2406.00943] places temporal graph modeling in a continuous-time structural system, MP-SSM [2505.18728] incorporates state-space computation directly into message passing, and Graph Set Transformer [2606.05116] interleaves local and contextual modeling. The likely distinctive contribution here is the specific conjunction of persistent sample boundary reduction, exact finite-duration processing, fixed physical impulses, and the serial-subdivision/age-potential contract. The generic PSD proof and a reversal probe do not establish that this conjunction yields an advantage. ARGMix appears only as an uncited “paper-equation adaptation,” making its relevance and reproduction scope impossible to assess from the manuscript.  
   **What would address it —** Add a concise comparison table identifying which antecedents support changing operators, exact durations, persistent interfaces, serial insertion invariance, fixed-anchor source updates, and repeated nonlinear depth. Explicitly separate inherited mathematics from the new construction. Supply one controlled task in which interval order or serial-equivalent representation is central, with input- and budget-matched implementations of the closest alternatives. For biological utility, include the task-specific ancestry comparator motivated in Section 1 when feasible, or explain exactly why it cannot be matched. Give the ARGMix citation and sufficient adaptation details. A methods paper can justify novelty through a carefully stated new contract, but it still needs evidence that the contract solves a material inference problem.

6. **Issue — The ancestry comparison protocol lacks an inferential plan commensurate with the small number of independent parents and the number of architectural choices.**  
   **Where —** Section 5.1 and Section 5.2's checkpoint selection and metric reporting.  
   **Why it matters —** Splitting parents before windowing is the right design choice. Nevertheless, four validation parents are four independent units, regardless of eight windows, 128 coordinates, or many query haplotypes. Correlated labels across a genealogy and linkage across coordinates make point-level error bars inappropriate. Minimum validation NLL is used for checkpoint selection, and the same validation cohort accompanies multiple geometry, depth, rate-parameterization, and propagation comparisons. Without a frozen final selection and an independent evaluation cohort, apparent differences can reflect architecture search on a small validation sample. No number of training seeds, uncertainty method, primary endpoint, meaningful-effect threshold, or handling of multiple contrasts is given. Hashes bind artifacts but do not resolve these statistical issues.  
   **What would address it —** Declare a primary outcome and a small set of confirmatory contrasts before viewing final test outputs. Select the architecture using validation only, then evaluate a sufficiently large independent parent cohort under a frozen protocol. Report paired parent-level differences with an uncertainty method that treats parents as clusters, and show variation across training seeds without treating seeds or coordinates as independent biological replicates. Plan sample size around a meaningful NLL or accuracy difference. Either adjust confirmatory multiple tests or clearly identify the larger factorial analysis as exploratory. Report class-specific metrics and calibration alongside the overall mean because ancestry prevalence can conceal rare-class failures.

7. **Issue — The Chickenpox extension is not instantiated well enough to verify causal forecasting or the scope of the cross-domain claim.**  
   **Where —** Section 3's two-sided completed kernel and Section 5.2's final paragraph.  
   **Why it matters —** A symmetric kernel is appropriate for genomic interpolation and can also forecast when every source is historical. Symmetry itself is not leakage. However, the manuscript does not say whether each forecast uses a separate 52-week tile, where its anchors and requested readout lie, or how the initial readout embedding U^0(x) is constructed for an unobserved target week. It also omits the county graph's construction/weights, forecast source features, query/reference partition, loss, and aggregation of county errors. Applying the scan to a full chronological sequence would admit future information, whereas restricting it to a historical input window would be legitimate. The current text does not distinguish these implementations. With a fixed graph, the operators commute, so this dataset cannot test the central changing-operator order mechanism.  
   **What would address it —** Specify the forecast computation explicitly: source timestamps, target timestamp, historical window boundaries, graph construction, input channels, readout initialization, and information available to every baseline. Include an auditable causality check that perturbing observations after the forecast origin leaves that forecast unchanged. Define whether test evaluation is rolling one-step-ahead with observed previous weeks and frozen weights, and report metrics and uncertainty appropriate for correlated weekly targets. Compare the actual covariance-based graph-kernel predictor cited in the text, not only generic neural and ridge controls. Describe the resulting evidence as temporal applicability on fixed topology.

### Minor concerns

- Proposition 1 should explicitly define T as the solution of the homogeneous equation with generator −K and initial identity. State the finite coordinate interval and identity covariance at its left endpoint. The existing covariance proof otherwise appears correct, including noncommuting holds.
- Proposition 2 should state that the anchor set is nonempty and readout coordinates are at finite distance. Its exact-arithmetic convex-combination conclusion is well supported. The full learned network is not bounded merely because M is bounded, and the manuscript should preserve its current narrow wording.
- The zero-response fallback normalizes C, but the graph encoder's weighted-neighbor mean also needs a convention for zero-degree boundary nodes.
- Sections 2.2 and 3.2 introduce F_g^Q, U_g^R, and later U^0_{Q,a}, U^0_{R,a} without an explicit correspondence. Clarify which projections initialize the residual embeddings and which are only source signals.
- Separate mathematical invariance from numerical verification. The sentence saying implementation checks cover subdivision, permutation, gradients, self counting, and mass behavior does not report their tolerances, seeds, test cases, or observed error. Include a compact receipt or appendix. Figure 1 verifies one homogeneous operator probe, not all neural predictions or both subdivision operations.
- The reproducibility account needs a self-contained table of encoder depth/width, H, activations, optimizer, learning rates, stopping rules, seeds, batch construction, and positive-rate bounds or failure handling. “Records retain” these items is not a data/code availability statement accessible to a reader.
- Give the four ancestry abbreviations in full, and cite/version the demography model, stdpopsim resource, Relate procedure, and Chickenpox dataset source.
- Clarify whether graph operators are recomputed when the sampled interface or reference panel changes. The stated contract fixes that interface, and boundary response plus mean-diagonal normalization makes cohort composition scientifically relevant.
- The working draft has no conclusion or limitations section. A short limitations paragraph should distinguish exact structural guarantees, the observed synthetic probe, planned predictive tests, and biological interpretation.

### Verdict

**Major revision**. The construction has credible mathematical guarantees, but a reviewable ancestry observation model, independent predictive evidence, resolution validation, and properly specified comparative/forecasting protocols are needed before its scientific value and external validity can be judged.
