### Summary

The paper makes a coherent set of structural claims: electrical boundary reduction and integrated age resistances preserve two specified subdivision operations, ordered finite-anchor propagation defines a conditional PSD matrix-valued kernel, and reference-inclusive normalization makes each message a bounded convex combination. It then proposes repeated query updates as a candidate ancestry architecture and describes comparisons intended to attribute any eventual benefit. These are different evidential categories. The proofs support the stated exact-arithmetic properties, and Figure 1 supports a narrow numerical illustration of order sensitivity and subdivision invariance. There are no reported learned ancestry or forecasting outcomes. The abstract is commendably explicit that this is a working methods document with validation-only comparisons in progress, but its phrase "Matched controls separate age geometry, kernel depth, query coupling and positive-rate optimization" reads as an achieved attribution result. My stance is that the mathematical construction is plausible, its constituent ideas are appropriately acknowledged, and useful ancestry memory remains an untested hypothesis. I would not infer that hypothesis from the order probe or from the existence of a factorial protocol.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue — [Novelty] The contribution beyond established dynamic-graph kernels and graph state-space models needs a sharper statement and a genuinely comparable evaluation.**

   **Where:** Section 1, especially "Our candidate combines a fixed sample interface with changing boundary operators and exact genomic-duration processing," and Section 5.1's architectural baselines.

   **Why it matters:** The probability that graph topology can condition a space-time kernel should not move on this paper. Dynamic-graph kernel reconstruction already estimates attributes of vertices from other vertices at different times and explicitly accommodates changing topology [1612.03615]. GraphSSM derives a continuous-time graph system using Laplacian regularization [2406.00943], and MP-SSM integrates state-space computation into permutation-equivariant message passing for static and temporal graphs [2505.18728]. The manuscript cites these lines of work, so I do not find a fabricated "first" claim or an obvious misattribution of Kron reduction or the SDE covariance argument. The plausible new contribution is the particular fixed-sample, serial-subdivision-preserving ancestry construction with finite anchors and reference-inclusive source normalization. That is a potentially useful engineering and representation contribution, not evidence for new covariance theory. The text does not yet identify which part changes what an informed reader should believe, and "paper-equation adaptations" do not tell me whether a comparison preserves the prior methods' strengths. MP-SSM is discussed but is not identified among the explicit Section 5.1 adaptation baselines. ARGMix appears there without a reference or explanation.

   **What would address it:** State the novel proposition precisely and distinguish the new composition from established ingredients. Provide a comparison table covering interface persistence, changing operators, coordinate duration, finite-anchor action, reference conditioning, and subdivision guarantees. Define the adaptations and their departures from the originals. Compare against a covariance or temporal-kernel method using the same observed features, boundary operators, reference information, and evaluation positions. Either include MP-SSM under a fair adaptation or explain why it cannot answer the target comparison. Cite and define ARGMix.

2. **Issue — [Evidence — biological task / alternative explanations] The ancestry target and observed input bank are too underspecified to establish what biological inference is being attempted.**

   **Where:** Sections 1, 2.2, and 5.2, especially "external census-lineage targets for ANA, CHG, WHG and EHG" and the 47-feature bank mentioned in Section 4.2.

   **Why it matters:** A lineage's population identity at a specified historical census is a simulation-defined estimand. The paper does not give the census horizon, the mapping from demography to the four target classes, the handling of lineages that do not fit that mapping, or the reference panel's composition and ages. Those details determine whether local genealogy contains enough evidence to identify the target and whether the task represents ancient ancestry inference outside this simulator. The feature bank already includes source-conditioned common-ancestor times, reference fractions, nearest-reference votes, and "fixed kernel evidence." A model could largely learn a nonlinear calibration of these informative summaries, with temporal processing acting as ordinary denoising. That explanation is fully consistent with the draft and does not require the proposed query-memory mechanism. Removal of census nodes and hidden population annotations is a useful safeguard. It does not by itself define the features or establish which reference labels and source-conditioning operations are legitimate at deployment. I find no demonstrated label leakage in the text, but the paper alone cannot audit it. It also leaves the distinction between simulated genealogies treated as observed and Relate-reconstructed genealogies insufficiently explicit.

   **What would address it:** Define the target generation and census horizon, with a small worked example of the target and permitted observed evidence. Specify reference sampling and label provenance and enumerate all 47 features, including the fixed-kernel features and their hyperparameters. State separately which cohorts use simulator genealogies and which use inferred genealogies. Report summary-only and graph-local predictors, including the nearest-reference voting information already supplied to the network. Establish any deployment-oriented claim on reconstructed trees and state the scope of generalization across demographies and reference panels. For paired Relate comparisons, evaluate on a declared common coverage and coordinate set and document whether clipping changes tile endpoints and anchor locations.

3. **Issue — [Evidence — statistical rigor / pre-registered versus post-hoc] The protocol does not yet provide a reliable estimate of comparative performance or a complete account of selection.**

   **Where:** Section 5.2's 16 training parents, four validation parents, separately seeded 64-query cohort, and minimum validation-NLL checkpoint rule. Completed comparative tables, uncertainty estimates, and tuning histories are absent.

   **Why it matters:** Four independent validation parents, rather than windows or correlated haplotypes and coordinates, are the main units available for judging the initial ancestry comparison. Repeated checkpoint selection and multiple architecture, geometry, depth, and rate-parameterization comparisons all reuse those units. A validation improvement would therefore be evidence useful for engineering selection, not an unbiased estimate of generalization. The draft honestly describes validation-only cohorts and supplies no p-values, so there is no borderline-significance claim to reject. There are also no effect sizes, seed counts, parent-level dispersion, or learned results to assess. The 64-query cohort changes simulation seeds as well as interface size, so a difference between the two cohorts would not isolate the effect of query count. For chickenpox, the 79 reserved chronological test targets are a useful provision, but consecutive weekly errors will not generally be independent. The age scales 350 and 1400, the factor 3 in the resistance family, the 32-anchor resolution, and the initial rate schedule are specific design choices whose selection chronology is not given. The word "fixed" is not evidence that they were selected before inspecting comparative outcomes.

   **What would address it:** Preserve the validation-only status until the protocol is frozen. Report paired parent-level outcomes, training-seed variation, all attempted comparison arms, and tuning budgets. Give a dated record of when the scales, anchor count, rates, and decision criteria were chosen, identifying exploratory choices without retroactively calling them confirmatory. Use independent ancestry parents for a final evaluation or confine the conclusions explicitly to exploratory model selection. Vary query count within matched simulations when interpreting interface-size effects. Use dependence-aware uncertainty or rolling-origin evaluation for the forecasting study, and keep its reserved test period untouched until model selection is complete.

4. **Issue — [Reproducibility] The paper specifies the kernel algebra much better than it specifies the executable ancestry pipeline.**

   **Where:** Sections 2.2, 3.2, and 5.1–5.2. Public code, data-generation configurations, seeds, and complete training settings are absent from the manuscript.

   **Why it matters:** A competent graduate student could implement a family of models satisfying these equations but could not reproduce a specific learned comparison from the paper alone. The GNN's depth, width, precise neighbor update, feature formulas, readout initialization, gating dimensions, optimizer, learning rate, batching, training budget, early stopping, and baseline tuning are not provided. The coordinate unit is especially consequential: the response operators are dimensionless, so the rates in the exponent have reciprocal coordinate units. Figure 1 uses a 0-to-1 coordinate, while the ancestry cohort is described in megabases. Whether coordinates are bases, megabases, or normalized within each tile changes the effective smoothing horizon. A statement that hashes "bind each comparison" is promising bookkeeping, but no reader-facing hashes, configuration bundle, or code location is supplied. Relate 1.2.4 and stdpopsim 0.3.0 are useful version pins, not a complete recipe. Positive-edge assumptions also need a documented treatment of zero-length or numerically tied reconstructed branches.

   **What would address it:** Supply a compact reproducibility appendix and an accessible code/configuration archive. Include all feature definitions, simulation and split seeds, target construction, reference sampling, coordinate transformations and rate units, model dimensions, optimizer settings, initialization, selection budgets, and exact baseline adaptations. Provide hashes and manifests readers can actually check. Report numerical precision and the policy for inadmissible reconstructed resistances and rejected denominator masses. Connect the claimed implementation checks to commands and their recorded tolerances. Do not use a code link as a substitute for describing the main settings in the paper.

5. **Issue — [Limitations] The exact structural guarantees need to be kept separate from resolution, numerical stability, biological robustness, and practical scaling.**

   **Where:** Sections 3.3 and 4, with a dedicated limitations discussion absent.

   **Why it matters:** I did not find an algebraic error in Propositions 1–3 under their stated assumptions. The SDE certificate establishes PSD of the unnormalized conditional kernel, the Metzler argument establishes the bounded normalized message, and telescoping resistances plus fixed sources support the subdivision contract. Those are useful guarantees. They do not establish PSD of the row-normalized aggregation, bounded residual embeddings, or training stability. The text correctly distinguishes the normalized action from its underlying kernel, and that distinction should be preserved. "Exact" means exact for the chosen finite anchor measure. It does not quantify error relative to a continuous source field or establish that 32 anchors resolve short ancestry tracts or informative short tree intervals. The invariance proposition holds with tile endpoints, source anchors, features, and interface fixed. It says nothing about changing tile resolution or making the observed genealogies more accurate. Rejecting nonpositive or nonfinite mass acknowledges finite-arithmetic failure, but no frequency or parameter-range evidence is given. Dense spectral propagation grows quadratically in query count, and Schur/eigensystem preparation is listed as a separate cost without numerical measurements. A 64-query illustration cannot establish practicality on large ancestry panels.

   **What would address it:** Add an explicit limitations section preserving these distinctions. Provide an anchor-resolution study and a sampling or approximation rationale tied to ancestry tract scales. State the dependence on tile partition and show how short intervals are represented. Quantify numerical failure rates over the intended learned-rate range, and report end-to-end preparation, storage, training, and inference costs rather than only the spectral propagation term. Include reconstruction-error and demographic-mismatch limitations. If learned-rate or residual stability is not established, say so directly.

6. **Issue — [Evidence — distinguishing tests / baselines / ablations] Neither the existing probe nor the proposed controls yet distinguishes useful changing-graph query memory from simpler explanations.**

   **Where:** Figure 1, Section 4.2's reported probe, and the factorial controls in Section 5.1. This is the strongest concern.

   **Why it matters:** Figure 1 does distinguish ordered from reversed homogeneous propagation for the chosen noncommuting operators and demonstrates unchanged-hold subdivision numerically. It does not distinguish the proposed learned mechanism from an ordinary correctly ordered linear dynamical system. It has no fitted model, ancestry target, prediction error, or control showing when order sensitivity is useful. The probe's absolute Frobenius differences at three rates are not estimates of a predictive effect. The decreasing difference at larger rates is compatible with stronger attenuation of every propagator. The draft does not report the original propagator norm, multiple independently sampled sequences, or the selection rule for the illustrated sequence. There are no ancestry qualitative examples, selected or random, to audit.

   The planned comparisons are thoughtful but have sharply different interpretations. Full versus scalar propagation changes diagonal decay as well as off-diagonal coupling, which the authors explicitly acknowledge. The raw diagonal arm repairs that particular confound but changes the dynamics' gain and spectrum. The gain-matched arm matches a **homogeneous continuous-forcing DC limit**: for constant generators, transforming a source by `D K^{-1}` gives the diagonal system the same stationary forced response. The actual architecture uses finite impulses and changing generators. In general `exp(-D t) D K^{-1} S` is not `exp(-K t) S`, and per-hold gain matching does not match transient responses across operator changes. Its source transform also mixes current query signals, so it is specifically a control for transfer from previous states, not for all query interaction. An eventual full-model advantage could still be an advantage in transient smoothing rather than evidence that particular cross-query paths convey ancestry information. One versus two layers also changes parameter count, gates, and nonlinear updates. Parameter counts being "recorded" does not isolate those effects. Age versus scalar-age and the repeated-plain three-branch control are sensible proposed ablations, but no result yet tells us that either age geometry or depth is doing useful work. Identical initial rates in log-positive and softplus-positive models do not make their subsequent optimizer trajectories equivalent. Those comparisons can reveal parameterization sensitivity, not establish a biological mechanism.

   The planned Relate comparison could assess reconstruction robustness, but no outcome is reported. The chickenpox study could establish utility in a second forecasting domain, but its fixed topology makes its homogeneous graph operators commute. It cannot validate the changing-topology, noncommuting mechanism motivating the ancestry construction. At present the empirical case consists of one appropriately labeled algebraic illustration plus several uncompleted study descriptions. More protocol paragraphs do not turn that into evidence of ancestry utility.

   **What would address it:** Complete and report the key matched comparisons before claiming attribution. Include a parameter- and training-budget-matched query-local depth control, the strongest summary-only predictor, raw and gain-matched diagonal arms, repeated plain branches, scalar-age geometry, and the rate-parameterization comparison. Explain exactly which alternative each arm rules out and which it leaves open. Add controlled positive and negative tasks with known targets: for example, examples sharing the same bag of operators and source information but differing in ordered-path-dependent targets, alongside targets for which cross-query transfer and order contain no extra information. Demonstrate that the architecture detects the former without manufacturing a gain on the latter. For actual ancestry, quantify whether gains concentrate where query evidence across graph intervals adds information beyond the reference summaries, using a prospectively specified analysis rather than selected attractive cases. Show transient behavior under the finite anchor measure and describe the gain-matched control's scope honestly. Report full-model prediction invariance under both subdivision operations, not just a homogeneous propagator probe. A decisive test of the central hypothesis is more valuable than adding another loosely related dataset.

### Minor concerns

- **[Narrative — claim precision and cohesion]** The mathematical claims form one coherent theme. I found no performance claim in the abstract that is contradicted by the body. Replace "Matched controls separate ..." with wording such as "We specify matched controls designed to compare ..." until actual attribution results exist. Keep "working methods document" visible wherever this draft is circulated.
- **[Novelty — attribution]** The explicit credit to Kron reduction, Node Decimation Pooling, and linear-process covariance theory is appropriate. No additional firstness objection arose from the cited/retrieved prior work. Explain whether the subdivision contract itself is the intended formal contribution or a design requirement established by composition.
- **[Presentation — equations]** Section 2.2 introduces `F_g^Q` and `U_g^R`, but Section 3.2 initializes `U^0_Q` and `U^0_R` without stating their exact relationship. Define this once. Use distinct notation for the interface set `S` and the branch source `S_{ell,s,h,a}` where possible. Initialize the arbitrary-readout base embedding explicitly.
- **[Presentation — figure interpretation]** Figure 1 is readable and its caption candidly identifies an untrained structural illustration. The common 0-to-1 heatmap scale makes most off-diagonal structure hard to inspect because the diagonal dominates. An off-diagonal inset or commutator statistic would make the reason for the order effect visible. State the coordinate units, random seed or construction rule, and relative as well as absolute propagator differences. The logarithmic error axis is otherwise appropriately labeled.
- **[Presentation — reader orientation]** An unfamiliar genetics reader needs a concrete explanation of a census-lineage ancestry label and why old branches can connect populations that are distinct at that horizon. A small worked example would do more than adding terminology.
- **[Evidence — cherry-picking]** No trained qualitative ancestry examples are presented, so there is no positive or negative cherry-picking finding for ancestry predictions. Selection of the single structural sequence is unspecified. Report how it was chosen and whether similar order behavior occurs across independent constructions.
- **[Evidence — pre-registration]** No preregistration or dated protocol is presented in the manuscript. This is not evidence of misconduct. It means eventual results on the described engineering cohorts should be labeled exploratory unless their selection history establishes otherwise.
- **[Evidence — statistical significance and quality versus quantity]** There are no p-values or comparative error bars to assess. The numerical tolerances in the order probe are deterministic numerical checks, not statistical evidence of a learned effect. The case currently rests on one narrow structural probe, not on multiple independently converging empirical tests.
- **[Evidence — temporal evaluation]** The chickenpox target counts sum to 470, consistent with 522 observations and a 52-week input history. Training-only normalization and historical-week inputs are appropriate. I find no explicit future-target leakage in that description. Document timestamp boundaries and rolling input construction so readers can verify this property.

### Belief update

I update positively, but narrowly, on the feasibility of a compositional representation contract: with a fixed observed interface, invariant features, fixed anchors, positive integrated resistances, and deterministic evaluation, subdivision invariance can coexist with noncommuting ordered graph propagation and bounded normalized messages. The proofs justify this update more than Figure 1 does. I make essentially no update on whether the architecture improves local ancestry inference, whether its age bank helps beyond reference summaries, whether repeated query transfer is responsible for any gain, or whether it is competitive with strong dynamic-graph/state-space alternatives. No learned comparative result is supplied. I also do not update on transfer from a simulated census-label task to practical ancient ancestry inference.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the structural construction is coherent, but the central distinguishing test of useful changing-graph query memory is absent, and the proposed controls do not yet resolve the main alternative explanations.
