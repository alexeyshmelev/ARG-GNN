### Summary

The draft asks whether marginal genealogies can support useful contextual prediction while their anonymous ancestors change and sampled individuals persist. It combines Kron-reduced observed tree geometry, an integrated age-resistance bank, independently encoded observed reference sources, and repeated nonlinear query updates through chronological matrix kernels at fixed physical anchors. Its strongest argument is a coherent representation contract: the covariance construction establishes PSD for the underlying symmetric block kernel, the matching reference-inclusive ones scan bounds messages, and electrical series equivalence plus the semigroup law establishes the stated subdivision invariances. These arguments appear sound under the declared assumptions. There is no headline empirical superiority result: the manuscript explicitly describes ongoing validation-only comparisons, and the frozen evidence states that the new anchor prototype has no completed comparative study. This is therefore a preliminary methods/protocol review, assessing what must be established before the construction can support a full AAAI-level research article.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue — The planned ancestry study has too narrow an observation and population regime to establish external validity.**
   **Where —** Section 5.2, the single AncientEurope_4A21 demography, four ancestry classes, fixed reference panel, and paired Relate pipeline; frozen evidence truthful_scope_notes.
   **Why it matters —** The strongest possible application claim is that a persistent sampled interface provides robust ancestry context despite imperfect inferred genealogies. The stated design instead primarily assesses one simulator, one reconstruction pipeline, observed phased variants, supplied sample ages, and a declared recombination map. Independent simulation seeds change realizations, not the demographic model or observation assumptions. The separately simulated Q8 and Q64 cohorts are also not a paired interface-size comparison, as the supplement correctly acknowledges. No present evidence establishes robustness to panel composition, uncertain ages, phasing errors, different recombination maps, or different demographic histories. These are consequential changes to both the source summaries and the reduced operators.
   **What would address it —** Freeze a separate ancestry test cohort after architecture selection, with enough independent parent replicates to estimate paired effects. Include at least one meaningful demographic or observation shift, and controlled perturbations of reference number/composition, age estimates, and genealogy reconstruction. To measure interface-size effects, subsample a common simulated cohort with a stated panel policy rather than compare scores from independently simulated cohorts. Report per-class performance and ancestry-boundary errors alongside parent-level NLL and calibration. If real-data evaluation lacks reliable ancestry ground truth, state that limit explicitly rather than equate real-data application with validated accuracy.

2. **Issue — The current evidence cannot yet quantify the anchor method's benefit, uncertainty, or reliability after extensive validation reuse.**
   **Where —** Abstract and Section 5.2; evidence.json records one-seed exploratory ancestry studies with 16 training and four validation parents, no generated final ancestry test, and an unfinished anchor comparison.
   **Why it matters —** The authors are appropriately candid that this is unfinished work. That candor does not supply the empirical evidence needed for a research article. Repeated architectures and checkpoint selection on four validation parents make the same validation scores unsuitable for an eventual unbiased advantage claim. Windows, haplotypes, and genomic coordinates from a parent are dependent observations, not additional independent replicates. The earlier inferred-boundary pilot reports validation NLL 0.937826 for boundary_full and 0.890872 for boundary_kernel, while the separate architecture-control pilot reports 0.920289 for semigroup_full and 0.893785 for semigroup_diagonal. These are exploratory precursor results, not results for this anchor architecture and not conclusive negative findings, but they prevent assuming that full graph propagation will prove advantageous. The implementation audit and passing tests establish contracts rather than predictive effects.
   **What would address it —** Before opening final tests, declare the primary endpoint, main paired contrasts, model/seed selection rule, practical minimum effect, and how exploratory families will be distinguished from confirmatory comparisons. Choose independent-parent test counts from pilot variability rather than coordinate counts. Report paired parent-level differences with uncertainty and separate variation across training seeds from variation across simulated parents. Address multiple confirmatory comparisons through a limited prespecified contrast family or an appropriate correction. For forecasting, use uncertainty procedures that respect temporal dependence instead of treating overlapping county-week targets as independent. Preserve the present separation between exploratory results and final evaluation.

3. **Issue — The incremental contribution beyond established graph reduction, graph kernels, and sequence models is not yet sufficiently characterized.**
   **Where —** Section 1 and Propositions 1–3 in Section 4.
   **Why it matters —** The draft properly credits Kron reduction, Node Decimation Pooling, graph state-space models, and linear-process covariance theory, and makes no unsupported first-method claim. Nevertheless, a correct synthesis is not automatically a substantial methodological advance. Romero et al., *Kernel-based Reconstruction of Space-time Functions on Dynamic Graphs* [1612.03615v2], already formulate space-time kernels for time-varying topology and batch/online estimators. Nikitin et al., *Non-separable Spatio-temporal Graph Kernels via SPDEs* [2111.08524v3], derive graph kernels from heat and wave equations to capture space-time interaction. Their inspected abstracts do not establish that either method contains this exact anchor neural construction, but they do establish that changing topology and graph-conditioned temporal kernels are existing ideas. The PSD certificate and serial-subdivision proof are convincing applications of established facts. The potentially distinctive part is their combination with a fixed sampled interface and nonlinear anchor-only source updates, whose additional value remains unresolved.
   **What would address it —** State one precise contribution claim and compare its assumptions, input interface, nonlinear update mechanism, interval processing, invariances, and computational costs against the nearest methods. Explain why the persistent boundary reduction and fixed-source/readout separation solve a problem those methods do not already solve. Support that difference through either a substantive theoretical result about representation/expressivity or decisive matched empirical evidence. Add a documented dynamic-graph kernel comparator and the stated covariance-based comparator where their task assumptions apply. Avoid treating a generic Transformer or an untrained paper-equation adaptation as a reproduced specialized ancestry method.

4. **Issue — The factorial controls do not fully isolate nonlinear kernel depth or the value of propagated query context.**
   **Where —** Section 5.1, especially the depth comparison and gain-matched diagonal control; frozen plain anchor configuration and integration audit.
   **Why it matters —** The improved controls correctly acknowledge that scalar propagation changes decay as well as coupling, and that homogeneous DC-gain matching is narrower than pointwise matching. However, depth adds gates, projections, and normalization parameters. Recording their count does not separate an advantage of repeated kernel context from an advantage of extra local nonlinear capacity. All modes already use a spatial source GNN and reference-relation summaries. Moreover, D K^{-1} mixes query sources instantaneously in the gain-matched diagonal mode. That mode removes transfer between previous query states, but does not remove all query-to-query information exchange. Full versus diagonal outcomes therefore require a more precise mechanism interpretation than "graph coupling helps."
   **What would address it —** Include a capacity-matched local residual control and a same-anchor attention or ordinary temporal model with the same source encoder, reference inputs, readout policy, and training budget. Add a frozen or explicitly noncontextual second-source-layer control to test whether updated query context itself matters. Specify exactly which information paths each control retains. Compare realistic tuning budgets, convergence, and parameter/runtime tradeoffs, and interpret gains only in terms of the paths changed by the relevant comparison.

5. **Issue — The age geometry has a valid invariance construction but an unvalidated biological motivation and scale choice.**
   **Where —** Section 2.3, particularly scales 350 and 1400 generations and the claim that older branches transmit evidence across ancestry-distinct populations; age qualification summarized in the integration audit.
   **Why it matters —** The derivative of the stated potential gives the bounded positive resistance density, and the Loewner bounds and telescoping argument are consistent. Those facts do not show that this weighting improves information about the census-lineage target, or that the selected scales transfer across demographies and reconstruction error. The qualification sample consists of five adjacent graphs from one training parent per input kind. Its strict tau350 reference-grounding counts are only 1/64 in each sampled oracle graph and 0 or 1/64 in the inferred graphs. This does not refute a smooth resistance weighting, which is not a hard cutoff, but it cannot serve as population-level support for the proposed ancestry mechanism. Learned global rates can also absorb attenuation, making geometry and effective memory length difficult to interpret without the scalar-age control and fitted-parameter evidence.
   **What would address it —** Explain the scale choices independently of final evaluation and freeze any selection rule. Complete plain, age, and matched scalar-age comparisons on the same parents and observation pipeline. Measure non-scalar geometry departures and reference grounding across the full cohort with parent-level summaries, and stratify effects by reference coalescence times and ancestry boundaries. Assess age/topology perturbations and alternative demographies. Report final rates and branch weights together with predictive contrasts, and distinguish a useful engineering feature from a demonstrated biological mechanism.

6. **Issue — Exactness for a finite anchor measure leaves source-resolution and tile-boundary effects unmeasured.**
   **Where —** Sections 3.2–3.3, fixed A=32 midpoint anchors per supplied tile, zero exterior scan states, and the fixed-endpoint assumptions of Proposition 3.
   **Why it matters —** The scan is exact for the declared anchor impulses; that is a real and properly scoped statement. It is not exact continuous-source integration, an anchor-refinement guarantee, or invariance to changing the tile partition. A source graph that contains no anchor contributes no direct source impulse, although its operator still affects propagation. Splitting parents into eight independently processed tiles resets context, changes the source grid, and may create boundary effects that are unrelated to ancestry. Nonlinear depth can make these effects more consequential. Redundant-record subdivision invariance therefore does not establish robustness to practical changes in spatial resolution or tiling.
   **What would address it —** Evaluate several anchor counts and shifted midpoint grids, report the fraction of observed graph intervals directly sampled by anchors, and assess errors as a function of distance to tile boundaries and ancestry changes. Compare a common set of readouts under alternative tile lengths, overlapping tiles, or a declared cross-tile context policy. Give exact coordinate units and normalization conventions for durations and rates. Keep the finite-measure exactness claim, but quantify the accuracy/cost and partition sensitivity of the chosen measure.

7. **Issue — The cross-domain study currently evaluates a related predecessor rather than a specified adaptation of this fixed-anchor method.**
   **Where —** Section 5.2's Chickenpox paragraph; inspected configs/chickenpox_boundary_pilot_v1.json and src/arggnn/boundary_chickenpox.py.
   **Why it matters —** The supplied Chickenpox adapter constructs a BoundaryMemoryModel with one-sided memory and a final observed-interval readout. Its configuration explicitly identifies an independent fixed-topology applicability pilot. The present paper instead develops repeated anchor-only query-source updates and a completed bidirectional kernel. A forecast from the earlier adapter cannot directly establish that this new mechanism transfers across domains. A fixed county graph also has no changing anonymous interiors, so it cannot validate the representation motivation. The reserved 79 test targets are useful, but their existence does not resolve the method mismatch.
   **What would address it —** Specify and evaluate an actual fixed-anchor forecasting adaptation using only the 52 observed historical weeks, with an explicit source/readout policy and treatment of the forecast coordinate. Explain whether the symmetric kernel is used only inside observed history or replaced by a causal construction, and which theoretical statements then apply. Compare the same architecture against seasonal, ridge, neural, and documented covariance-based controls on a frozen protocol. Alternatively, present the current Chickenpox results strictly as predecessor evidence and narrow the new method's claims to ancestry until its cross-domain evaluation is completed.

8. **Issue — Practical numerical and computational limits are not yet demonstrated at the scale implied by sampled genealogical interfaces.**
   **Where —** Section 3.3's dense spectral cost, Section 4.1's nonpositive/nonfinite-mass rejection, and the Q8/Q64 studies in Section 5.2.
   **Why it matters —** Dense propagation is quadratic in query count, and Schur/eigensystem preparation and dense cached operators are separate costs rather than removed costs. Small interfaces may not predict behavior for larger reference/query panels or long regions with many genealogies. Exact-arithmetic positivity also does not supply a useful finite-precision mass lower bound: high learned rates and distant anchors can underflow at non-anchor readouts. Rejecting such a case makes failure visible, which is preferable to silently changing the operator, but does not establish a usable operating range. The audit's real Q64 forward/backward check supports one bounded configuration, not a general numerical or scaling result.
   **What would address it —** Report end-to-end preparation, cache storage, training/inference time, and peak memory while varying query/reference count, graph count, anchor count, and tile length. Include dependency versions, source-feature definitions, exact preparation policies, and runnable artifact instructions so the documented hashes can be used by an outside researcher. Measure minimum mass, rejection frequency, and exponent ranges across fitted models and shifts, with float32/float64 sensitivity. If failures occur, define and validate a stable implementation or a supported domain restriction without silently inserting a normalization floor. Compare computational costs under equivalent inputs and prediction coverage.

### Minor concerns

- Sections 2.2 and 3.2 introduce both the initial bounded projections F_g^Q, U_g^R and the later U^0, q, and r notation. State whether the initial equations are actually used by this architecture or are explanatory remnants, and identify the dimensions of each embedding.
- Define the 47 selected observed features and all reference-category aggregation rules in a reproducible appendix. The descriptions of "fixed kernel evidence" and source-conditioned common-ancestor summaries are insufficient to reconstruct them from the article alone.
- The distinction between the PSD underlying block kernel and the normalized, reference-forced neural action is correct. Preserve it consistently, and explain what practical role the PSD property plays once row normalization and nonlinear updates are applied.
- Proposition 3 appropriately conditions on invariant observed features and fixed tile endpoints. Present those limitations alongside the abstract's invariance claim and explicitly exclude changes in the sample/reference set or anchor resolution.
- Section 5.1 names ARGMix without an accompanying reference or concise definition in the shown bibliography. Supply the exact source and adaptation scope before using it as a comparative baseline.
- Add a data/code availability statement and a brief conflicts/ethics statement appropriate to simulated human genealogies, public disease counts, and any future human-data use. Their absence is a reporting gap, not evidence of an ethical violation.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — The preliminary draft contains a coherent and apparently correct mathematical construction, but a full research article still requires a sharper contribution argument, direct anchor-method evidence, independent statistical evaluation, and convincing robustness beyond the present simulator and predecessor studies.
