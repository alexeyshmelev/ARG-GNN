# Interval-tree Haar semigroup: novelty audit

Date: 2026-10-16. Status: literature exploration only; **reject the generic model-novelty claim**. No implementation, registration, training, simulation, GPU work, scoring, or utility evidence. This review does not alter the separate H050 candidate or its pending full scores.

## Verdict

The proposed map

`U_G(delta) = H_G diag(exp(-delta * a_G)) H_G^T = exp(-delta L_G)`,

with `L_G = H_G diag(a_G) H_G^T`, is a learned Haar spectral filter restricted to a contractive linear semigroup. Applying it along a changing tree sequence, with genotype assimilation only at real SNP events, is a linear, closed-form specialization of established hybrid graph neural differential equations and switching-network dynamics. Semigroup subdivision invariance, order sensitivity from noncommuting generators, and fast hierarchical transforms are useful properties, but are not new contributions. Combining these ingredients for local ancestry could be an application; the present proposal does not establish a novel ancestry mechanism or improvement over the strong GNN+SNP+BiGRU baseline.

Diffusion-wavelet methods can construct a basis from a prescribed diffusion operator; the proposal instead constructs an operator from a tree basis. That direction alone is insufficient novelty because learned Haar-diagonal filtering and hierarchical tree-supported semigroups already exist. A switching ODE describes evolution; a CDE additionally describes driving signals/interpolation. A finite-state, piecewise-constant generator and event jumps fit the hybrid/switching formulation directly. By the Dyson/Picard expansion, a linear controlled evolution is also a matrix representation of iterated integrals/path signatures. Noncommutative ordering is not a new path-signature mechanism.

## Correct mathematical claims

Let the same `n` leaves align across trees. For a binary rooted tree, an internal split `v` has children with leaf sets `A,B`, sizes `p,q`, and `m=p+q`. Define a unit contrast as `sqrt(q/(p*m))` on A, `-sqrt(p/(q*m))` on B, and zero elsewhere. Together with `phi_0=1/sqrt(n)`, these `n-1` contrasts form an orthonormal leaf Haar basis. Unary ancestors add no independent contrasts. No internal ancestor identities are needed.

1. **Subdivision invariance.** For a fixed, deterministic generator, `U_G(s)U_G(t)=U_G(s+t)` for nonnegative lengths. Insert any number of artificial boundaries within an unchanged operator interval and the final hidden state is identical, up to floating-point error. This also holds in the full predictor if the subdivision adds no observation update, readout feedback, normalization, stochastic resampling, or changed generator. Identical topology alone is insufficient when branch-time features/rates differ. Rates depending on segment length or recomputed hidden state generally invalidate this frozen-exponential argument. This is invariance to representation refinement, not invariance to arbitrary changes of genomic distances. Use an additive physical or genetic distance measure consistently.
2. **Equivariance.** Under a simultaneous permutation `P` of leaf rows, require `H_{PG}=P H_G S` and `D_{PG}=S^T D_G S`, where `S` is a signed column permutation. Then `U_{PG}=P U_G P^T`. Rate prediction must respect corresponding split permutations and child swaps, and ignore arbitrary internal IDs. A sign-sensitive rate predicted from a Haar coefficient can break child-swap invariance. For multifurcations, different contrast bases involve full rotations, not only signs: tie the spectrum within each child-contrast subspace or prove stronger covariance. Arbitrary binary resolutions and per-vector spectra do not have automatic invariance.
3. **Contraction.** Nonnegative rates yield `||U_G(delta)||_2=exp(-delta min a_G)<=1`; the same bound applies to Frobenius norms of leaf-by-channel states. Products are nonexpansive. If the constant mode rate is zero, constants and the leaf mean are preserved, so the full map is **not strictly contractive**. Positive contrast rates contract the zero-mean subspace. These guarantees apply to propagation, not to unrestricted genotype-update cells or the full network. Strong decay can erase useful memory.
4. **Chronology.** Generators from different trees can fail to commute. To second order, reversing two short intervals changes the map by `s*t*[L_2,L_1]`. Different bases do **not necessarily** imply noncommutativity: equal nonconstant rates give the same `L=a*(I-11^T/n)` for every tree. A chronological product is generally nonsymmetric even though each factor is symmetric positive definite, and therefore is not automatically a Mercer kernel or one symmetric diffusion.
5. **Complexity.** For a supplied binary hierarchy, bottom-up weighted means/contrasts, diagonal decay, and top-down reconstruction cost `O(n*d)` time and `O(n*d)` working storage for hidden width d. This remains true for an unbalanced tree if descendant leaves are not repeatedly enumerated. No dense `n*n` matrix is required. It is not a constant-cost sequence algorithm: T intervals cost `O(T*n*d)`, before SNP jumps, rate encoders, and backpropagation. Dense d-by-d neural updates add their own costs; naive sequence activation storage scales with event count. High-degree multifurcations need an efficient contrast transform or tied-subspace projector for a linear claim.

## PSD is not a Markov or ancestry guarantee

Even with `a_0=0`, arbitrary nonnegative Haar spectra do not guarantee entrywise nonnegative propagators. For three leaves, take `phi=(1,1,1)/sqrt(3)`, `psi_r=(1,1,-2)/sqrt(6)`, `psi_c=(1,-1,0)/sqrt(2)`, with rates `(0,6,1)`. Then `L_12=6/6-1/2=1/2>0`, so `U_12(delta)=-delta/2+O(delta^2)<0` for sufficiently small positive delta. Every eigenvalue of L is nonnegative, but this cannot mix ancestry probability vectors as nonnegative weights.

For `Q=-L` to be a continuous-time Markov generator, require `L*1=0` and all off-diagonal `L_ij<=0`. One established sufficient hierarchical parameterization is `L=sum_v c_v*(I_v-P_v)`, `c_v>=0`, where `I_v` restricts to a clade and `P_v=1_v 1_v^T/|v|` averages within it. Its Haar rate is the sum of ancestor coefficients and its off-diagonal entries are nonpositive. This positivity repair is known hierarchical diffusion, not a residual novelty claim. Even positive stochastic mixing over leaves is not a derivation of ancestry-class transition probabilities or a calibrated local-ancestry posterior; a final softmax cannot supply that biological interpretation.

## Sole residual research question, not a claimed contribution

Could one construct and prove an **exact lazy Haar-coordinate propagator for ARG/tree-sequence edge changes**, with clade-local rate functions, whose propagation work is `O((n + sum_j r_j + S*n)*d)` plus topology bookkeeping, rather than `O((T+S)*n*d)`? Here S is the number of genuine genotype-update events and r_j is the number of local binary rotations used to change hierarchy j into j+1. A local rotation changes only two contrast coordinates; unchanged coordinates could decay lazily until touched. This is an algorithmic question, not a new model class. It requires a precise dynamic data structure, an exactness/complexity proof including topology changes and leaf materialization, and a dedicated priority search for dynamic wavelet/tree algorithms. Globally conditioned rates can invalidate reuse; a low-rank generator difference alone does not establish the proposed cost. No such algorithm or priority claim is established here. If that work is not pursued, there is no remaining defensible novelty claim in the current proposal.

## Falsifiable prospective controls

No controls below were run.

- **Exactness and representation:** full recomputation versus the residual lazy algorithm, paired on the same supplied sequences and weights; artificial interval subdivision, internal-ID renumbering, leaf permutations, and child swaps must preserve corresponding states/predictions to numerical tolerance. Include highly unbalanced and multifurcating cases. A claimed speedup must include basis/rate/topology and SNP materialization costs, and should disappear as changes become global or SNPs become dense.
- **Matched utility:** retain the same whole-tree/SNP encoder, BiGRU/readout, data splits, optimization, and seeds. Compare no added flow, a capacity-matched topology-blind mean-preserving semigroup `P0+exp(-lambda*delta)*(I-P0)`, and the hierarchical spectrum. Use held-out ancestry/boundary metrics and paired uncertainty at the independent genomic/sample unit. Failure to beat the topology-blind clock control rejects a tree-specific mechanism; no generic-memory claim follows from development accuracy alone.
- **Order and metric mechanism:** within SNP-free gaps, compare chronological tree-duration pairs against an order-agnostic integrated-generator control, and separately a control that redistributes tree dwell times while keeping each SNP gap's total distance and observation events fixed. Keep SNP encodings/readouts unchanged. Failure of chronology or dwell-time information to add held-out value rejects the proposed order/interval explanation even if the architecture trains well. These are prospective diagnostic controls, not authorization to launch runs.
